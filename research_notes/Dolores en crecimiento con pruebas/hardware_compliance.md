## Key Question 2: What do creators say about certification confusion, cost and delays (verbatim, 2025–2026 prioritized)?

### Takeaway
First-person community evidence (HN, forums, maker blogs) consistently frames certification as a $5k–$25k+ "nightmare" that small sellers either absorb or skip ("rolling the dice"); the freshest first-person thread is a Sept 2026 Ask HN by a one-person German company trying to work out CRA obligations. I could not open Reddit/HN/EEVblog pages, so most quotes below are as relayed in search snippets, not confirmed verbatim.

### Cited Findings
- Hacker News (thread July 2024, id 40923337, reached via snippet): "External certification and compliance tests for hardware typically range from $5,000 to $25,000 USD depending on the number of runs required to achieve certification, what needs to be checked, and the industry involved (including battery management safety, medical, aerospace, FCC and/or CE, RED and/or others)." Also: certification is "a high cost burden for small startups and hobbyists who aren't paying themselves. Small-scale prototype sellers on sites like Tindie are essentially rolling the dice hoping not to get fined, and the cost of $5,000-$10,000+ per product makes it impractical to certify multiple niche designs." — [HN comment 40923337](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40923337); sibling comment: "If the project is actually just a hobby, then CE and probably FCC testing is not..." required — [HN comment 40924484](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40924484) (snippet truncated)
- Hacker News, "Ask HN: Is anyone else preparing for the EU Cyber Resilience Act?" (posted ~Sept 2026 per snippet "about a month ago"): OP "runs a one-person GmbH in Germany and is selling firmware for an off-the-shelf handheld device (offline, with no WiFi or network stack compiled in, updates by reflashing over USB)". Points raised in thread (as relayed): "There's no size threshold—a one-person company carries the same obligations as a large one"; "The open source exemption only applies to software supplied outside commercial activity; firmware published to support a product the company sells is plainly commercial, regardless of what is charged for it"; "if the product isn't in Annex III, it's self-assessment: no notified body, no fee, nothing filed, nobody approves anything. The work seems to be a handful of documents written once. Basically stick the CE label on by yourself"; "Every security update must stay available for 10 years after it's issued or the rest of the support period, whichever is longer—potentially extending into the 2040s for a product launched in 2027." — [Ask HN 49520688](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49520688)
- Hacker News (2018): "The Orange and Nano Pis are not FCC-certified, so they're not likely to be integ[rated]..." into commercial products — [HN 16443153](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16443153)
- Maker blog "Certifiably great" (ESP32-based retro-computer project) documents a maker's own CE/FCC certification journey — [esp32zx.substack.com](https://esp32zx.substack.com/p/certifiably-great) (could not open; content unknown beyond title and that it concerns certifying a small ESP32 product)

- Bambu Lab community thread (2025, CyberBrick Kickstarter): shipping delayed; "a recent fire at a logistics warehouse in Hong Kong led to increased scrutiny from customs and logistics providers, especially for products containing batteries, and the project was awaiting approval before proceeding with shipment" — [Bambu Lab forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/is-the-kickstarter-shipping-delayed/169532) (battery/transport compliance, not CE per se)

### Inferences
- The pain is persistent rather than new: the same confusions (module pre-certification, FCC≠CE, cost vs. unit volume, Tindie sellers skipping certification) recur from 2018 to 2026 threads. What is new in 2025–2026 is the CRA layering a documentation/SBOM/vulnerability-process burden on top, which is exactly the kind of thing a written roadmap can address.
- The Ask HN thread (Sept 2026) is direct evidence that a solo hardware/firmware seller's bottleneck is *understanding which documents to write*, not lab access: "The work seems to be a handful of documents written once." That matches a "documentation pack bought online" offer.

## Key Question 5: What does the Cyber Resilience Act add for connected hardware sellers, and are small makers aware?

### Takeaway
The CRA (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, in force 10/11 Dec 2024) makes cybersecurity a CE-marking condition for every connected product: self-assessment and technical documentation for ~90% of products, vulnerability-handling and 24h/72h incident reporting from 11 Sept 2026, full application 11 Dec 2027, with no size exemption and only non-commercial OSS exempt. Awareness among small makers is visible but patchy: a Sept 2026 Ask HN shows a solo seller only now working out obligations; Hackaday coverage dates from 2023 (OSS threat framing), and Espressif/consultant guides for ESP32 makers only appeared in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- "The Cyber Resilience Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/2847) sets mandatory cybersecurity requirements for hardware and software products with a data connection sold in the EU. The regulation was adopted on October 23, 2024, and entered into force on December 10, 2024." ### Inferences
- For a small connected-hardware seller the CRA adds, on top of LVD/EMC/RED/RoHS: a cybersecurity risk assessment, secure-by-default requirements, an SBOM/technical documentation set, a vulnerability disclosure and handling process, a support-period commitment, and reporting to ENISA/CSIRT within 24h/72h from Sept 2026 — all of which are documentation/process deliverables suited to a written pack (the Ask HN consensus: "a handful of documents written once").
- Awareness is bifurcated: the OSS community debated the CRA in 2023; commercial micro-sellers (Tindie/Shopify/Kickstarter type) appear to be discovering obligations only in 2026, as the Ask HN and the sudden appearance of ESP32-targeted guides suggest. This timing (Sept 2026 → Dec 2027) is a demand tailwind for the next ~15 months.
